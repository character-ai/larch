## Approach

Add a small two-pass fence gate around the existing `/issue` batch parser.

1. In `parse_issue_input`, split once into `lines`.
2. Pass 1 records only balanced fenced-code ranges.
   - Opener: `line.strip()` starts with at least three backticks or tildes.
   - Opener may have an info string, such as `markdown`.
   - Closer: same marker character, marker length at least the opener length, and no suffix after stripping.
   - Unclosed openers do not create a range. They degrade to plain text.
3. Pass 2 keeps the current parser state machine.
   - For fenced line indices, skip `OOS_HEADING_RE`, `PLAIN_HEADING_RE`, and `consume_oos_field`.
   - Let fenced lines append as body content for the active item.
   - Do not change unfenced `### ` behavior or the `parse-input:` breadcrumb.
4. Keep this surgical. Do not add flags, parser modes, or a broad Markdown parser.

## Edge cases

- A longer closer closes a shorter opener.
- Backtick and tilde fences do not cross-close each other.
- A marker-only opener can be closed by the next marker-only line of the same character and sufficient length.
- Unclosed opener at EOF does not swallow later boundaries.
- Fenced OOS metadata-looking bullets stay body text while inside the fence.
- Unfenced `### ` lines keep the documented #2152 split behavior.

## Failure modes

- Naive toggle logic can merge later real issues into one item after an unclosed fence.
- Gating only `PLAIN_HEADING_RE` can still let OOS headings or OOS fields corrupt fenced payloads.
- Treating every marker-only line as a closer outside an active fence can miss a valid fence opener.
- Changing final-newline behavior can break existing body-file expectations.
