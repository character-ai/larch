Reviewing the `/design` skill flow and plan artifacts so framings align with the codebase.
Here are **six distinct framings** for issue #2828. Each stands alone; none is endorsed as “the” scope.

---

**1. Operator approval surface (comprehension at Gate C)**  
Treat the new step as making **human oversight** the primary win: the operator’s reading task at Step 4b (“Final Design Plan”), in-chat plan previews, and the published `larch:plan` block is to **approve scope and risk without re-parsing dense agent prose**. The feature is a mandatory, always-on **readability pass** on the same canonical plan that reviewers and `/implement` already consume—Strunk & White tightening plus dyslexia-friendly scaffolding (short sentences, simpler words, more headings/bullets)—while every path, identifier, fenced command, `diff_lines:` trailer, and `### NEW|UPDATED|REWRITTEN:` grammar stay byte-identical so mechanical downstream parsers are untouched.

---

**2. Accessibility layer on a precision-critical artifact**  
Frame this as **inclusive design documentation**: plans are currently optimized for agent-to-agent and implementer precision, which creates unnecessary friction for operators with mild dyslexia or high visual-processing load. The step adds an **accessibility scaffold** (sentence length caps, vocabulary simplification, structural air) on top of classic prose discipline, applied once to `plan.txt` / `composed-plan.md` and to accepted-OOS Description text before public filing—explicitly **not** a correctness gate: rewriter failure falls back to the original text and logs a Warning, because the contract is “easier to read,” not “different facts.”

---

**3. Dual-register translation (spec register → operator register)**  
Describe the work as a **register shift**, not a replan: Step 2b–4b produce a technically faithful implementation spec; the new step **translates only connective prose** between frozen code-reference tokens into operator-readable language while preserving a verbatim fact layer (paths, flags, commands, code fences). Value is measured by whether a non-implementer can skim Plan + Acceptance and OOS descriptions and answer “what will change and how we’ll know it worked” without wading through reviewer-register density—same semantic content, lower decoding cost per paragraph.

---

**4. Non-blocking UX polish in the finalize pipeline**  
Position the step alongside `redact-secrets.sh` as a **late-stage human-facing polish** in the Step 5 sequence: compose → **simplify** → validate (when applicable) → redact → `plan-block-write.sh`. It reshapes how the plan *reads* but must never reshape what `/implement` can *trust*—hence fail-open semantics, idempotency expectations, and strict exclusion of feature-context, dialectic, and brainstorm artifacts. Success is invisible when it works (one simplified canonical plan everywhere humans look); failure is visible only in `execution-issues.md`, not as a blocked `/design` run.

---

**5. Unified canonical plan for every downstream consumer**  
Frame around **artifact convergence**: today humans may see a plan in chat while GitHub carries `larch:plan`, and Step 3/5b/5c share `plan.txt`—the feature mandates **one simplified source of truth** flowing to every human-facing surface (chat display, `composed-plan.md`, issue body, and by inheritance the review panel and `/implement` preflight). The design problem is pipeline placement and contracts (`larch:plan` markers, `plan-block-write.sh`, command validator on unchanged fences) so simplification is a **single mandatory transform** rather than an optional “nice summary” diverging from what implementers execute.

---

**6. Cognitive-load restructuring (document architecture, not just word choice)**  
Emphasize **information architecture** over copy-editing alone: long prose blocks in plans and OOS descriptions force serial reading; the step’s job is to **re-chunk** content (more `##`/`###`, bullets, shorter paragraphs) under Strunk & White rules while leaving machine-parseable regions (file lists, fenced scripts, backticked tokens) as fixed islands. The reader benefit is **scanability**—finding acceptance criteria, touch points, and OOS scope by heading—reducing working-memory load for dyslexic and non-dyslexic operators alike, with OOS Description simplification extending the same readability standard to **public GitHub issues** filed at Step 5b.

---

These framings are compatible with your Round-1 bindings (both surfaces, style scope, always-on, fail-open, precision contract, plan + OOS only) but stress different “why” stories for the next design agent.
