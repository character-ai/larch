## Edge cases

- A prose rewrite can accidentally weaken normative language. Keep directive words when they carry behavior, such as rejected, persisted, default, required, exit codes, and strict `>`.
- The file mixes public flags, internal orchestration notes, and legacy flags. Compress each section, but do not merge them in a way that blurs public versus internal scope.
- `--approve`, `--manual`, `-m`, and `--hard` are rejection behavior. Do not make them sound accepted or deprecated-but-usable.
- The acceptance target applies to the flag reference text. The closure report will show a smaller overall design closure, but the percentage drop in the full closure will be much smaller.
- Skipping baseline regen or failing to commit it after regen passes `make lint-skill-closure-growth` on a shrink but fails `test_committed_baseline_matches_fresh_scan` in CI.

## Failure modes

- **Behavior drift in prose**: mitigated by comparing every flag/default/key before and after the edit.
- **Stale ratchet baseline**: mitigated by regenerating `python/skill-closure-baseline.json`, committing the refreshed file, and passing `make test-lint-skill-closure-growth` (or `python3 -m pytest python/tests/lint/test_lint_skill_closure_growth.py::test_committed_baseline_matches_fresh_scan`). `make lint-skill-closure-growth` is the growth-only check and is not sufficient shrink-path confirmation on its own.
- **Over-compression**: mitigated by preserving examples and contracts that prevent parser or plan-size ambiguity.
- **Markdown lint churn**: keep headings, bullets, and code spans valid. Do not add Mermaid fences.
