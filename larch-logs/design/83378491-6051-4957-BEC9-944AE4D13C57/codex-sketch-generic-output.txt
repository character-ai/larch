Implement this as two small orchestration scripts, not as new rebase or dirty-tree logic. `rebase-checkpoint-probe.sh` should be a thin state machine: parse `<step-prefix> <short-name>` plus optional `--base-remote/--base-ref`, emit exactly one breadcrumb, run `rebase-push.sh --no-push --skip-if-pushed --keep-on-conflict`, normalize its stdout/stderr into stable KVs, and only on non-conflict completion call the shared phantom warning helper path. On `rc=1`, emit `REBASE_OUTCOME=conflict` and `CONFLICT_FILES=...` and return control to `SKILL.md` unchanged for the existing Conflict Resolution Procedure; on `rc=3` emit `REBASE_OUTCOME=failed` plus `REBASE_ERROR`; on success emit `REBASE_OUTCOME=ok|skipped` with skip KVs and phantom KVs.

I’d factor the phantom behavior into `phantom-probe-with-warn.sh` and have `rebase-checkpoint-probe.sh` call it internally rather than duplicate append logic. That script should own `check-phantom-dirty.sh`, parsing `STATUS/REASON/PHANTOM_COUNT/PHANTOM_PATHS_FILE`, appending Warnings through `append-execution-issue.sh` for `phantom` and `unknown`, and surfacing append failures as non-fatal warning KVs. Files to modify are the two new `.sh` scripts and sibling `.md` docs, two focused harnesses, `Makefile`, `docs/linting.md`, `scripts/lint-foreground-markers.sh`, `scripts/test-implement-rebase-macro.sh`, and `skills/implement/SKILL.md` to replace the macro prose/call sites with foreground-marked wrapper invocations.

The main tradeoff is moving branching out of the prompt into shell contracts, so the KVs and exit codes need to be very explicit and pinned by tests. The riskiest edges are conflict behavior, append-warning failure handling, and accidental behavior drift from adding the `1.r` phantom probe; those should be covered directly in the offline harnesses with stubbed helper scripts. I would keep the wrapper deliberately boring: no fork detection, no conflict-resolution orchestration, no Step 0 coupling, and no broad SKILL.md rewrite beyond replacing the known fences.
