# review-points-overhaul Price non-accepted findings below silence

Make a non-accepted finding cost the reviewer slightly more than staying silent.

**Problem.** A `neutral` finding (≥1 YES but below threshold) scores `0`, identical to silence. But the neutral finding consumed three voter evaluations; silence consumed nothing. So the middle is **free**, and it functions as a penalty cushion: a reviewer rationally raises anything where `P(accept) &gt; P(reject)`, because a near-miss lands costlessly in the middle instead of at `−1`. That is exactly the borderline-fluff zone.

**Proposed change.** Make non-acceptance cost something uniformly. Either set `neutral` to a small negative (e.g. `−0.25`), or add a flat per-finding submission cost. This tightens the raise-threshold from `P(accept) &gt; P(reject)` toward `P(accept) &gt; 0.5`. Calibrate gently to avoid recall loss.

**Evidence.** 17.0% of in-scope findings land neutral. Note the maintainers already moved the legacy "all-exonerate = 0" outcome to "0 YES = −1"; this continues that trajectory of pricing the costless middle.

**Affected surfaces.** `python/voting.py` (`classify_result` → points), `docs/point-competition.md`, `skills/shared/voting-protocol.md`.

**Depends on.** Value-weighted points (land the base point structure first).
