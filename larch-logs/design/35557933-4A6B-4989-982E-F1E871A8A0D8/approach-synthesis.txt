### Tradeoffs and risks

**Lint precision** (Codex's main tradeoff): scanning every basename mention would false-flag contract prose (e.g., `ship-pr.sh` is referenced repeatedly in NEVER #16's WHY paragraph). Scanning only fenced shell blocks is cleaner.

**Reasoned call**: lint scans **fenced shell blocks only** (```bash ... ``` and equivalent), matching by basename at the leading-command position of any non-comment line inside the fence. Prose mentions of the basename outside fences are NOT flagged. This is the right tradeoff because prose mentions aren't invocations; only fenced blocks become Bash tool calls.

**Risk of accidentally touching Family A** (Codex): the audit MUST NOT modify Family A launch blocks (e.g., `sketch-launch.md`'s `run_in_background: true` instructions). The denylist is by Family B basename; Family A basenames (`launch-review.sh`, `launch-codex-implement.sh`, `launch-cursor-implement.sh`) are NOT on the denylist, so the lint cannot accidentally require foreground markers for those blocks.

**Monitor-tailed scripts**: the §4 exception list explicitly carves out scripts designed for `Monitor`-based tailing. None of the listed Family B basenames fall into this category today; the carve-out is for future-proofing.
