I’d treat this as a broken orchestration contract, not three isolated voter bugs. The novel fix is a “round transaction” boundary: Step 5 should not publish completion until `review-and-fix.sh` has committed a final round envelope, and voter tally should consume only finalized artifacts. The most suspicious cascade point is that nested scripts such as `scripts/dispatch-with-waterfall.sh` and `scripts/collect-agent-results.sh` call `larch_quiet_append_done_trap` while inheriting the top-level `LARCH_DONE_SENTINEL`; `scripts/lib-quiet.sh` guards by owner PID, but nested scripts can re-own the same sentinel and make `scripts/breadcrumb-monitor.sh` exit early. Give nested Family B scripts private sentinels, or explicitly unset `LARCH_DONE_SENTINEL`/`LARCH_STATUS_FILE` before synchronous nested calls unless the caller is the orchestrator-paired process.

Tradeoff: sentinel isolation is less flashy than “wait longer,” but it attacks the system-level failure that allowed redundant MAV and concurrent round 2. Waiting for voter `.done` files improves correctness but can increase latency when a voter is hung; keep timeout behavior explicit and degrade only after the timeout record exists. Crazy but plausible: make Step 5 append-only event-sourced, where every reviewer/voter/collector writes JSONL events and a single reducer derives round state; the monitor watches only the reducer’s terminal event. Even crazier: replace three separate voter CLIs with one “voter broker” long-lived process holding stable TTY/stdin and spawning model calls behind a local queue, so shell job lifetime stops being part of the correctness model.
