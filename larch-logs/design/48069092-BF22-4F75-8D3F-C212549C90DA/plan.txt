## Edge cases

- A prose edit can delete a literal that `scripts/test-design-structure.sh` or `scripts/test-implement-anti-polling-rule.sh` greps exactly. Keep those literals verbatim.
- Over-compression can weaken the anti-halt semantics. Keep all action rules explicit, especially empty-output notification handling, one-probe recovery, duplicate notification fingerprint handling, and the no-background-waiter ban.
- Regenerating the closure baseline before the markdown edit, or hand-editing it, can hide the intended token reduction or create a stale ratchet row.
- Running `make lint-skill-closure-growth` alone after a shrink can look green while `test_committed_baseline_matches_fresh_scan` still fails in CI because the committed baseline is stale.
- Wrapping long lines can inflate diff size. Prefer targeted sentence edits over whole-file reflow.

## Failure modes

- **Harness failure from missing literal**: restore the exact missing string, then compress nearby non-contract prose instead.
- **Closure ratchet failure**: rerun `make regen-skill-closure-baseline`, then rerun the ratchet. If it still fails, inspect the generated design row for unexpected growth.
- **Baseline freshness failure**: prose shrink lowers `closure_estimated_tokens` while one-directional `lint-skill-closure-growth` still passes. CI fails on `test_committed_baseline_matches_fresh_scan` until regen output is committed. Fix by rerunning `make regen-skill-closure-baseline`, committing `python/skill-closure-baseline.json`, then rerunning `make test-lint-skill-closure-growth`.
- **Token reduction too small**: make another local pass on repeated explanatory clauses only. Do not alter rule substance or protected literals to chase the percentage.
- **Unexpected consumer regression risk**: stop and inspect all direct references to `skills/shared/design-background-wait.md`; do not edit consumers because the approved scope excludes them.
