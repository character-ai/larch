## Dialectic Resolutions

### DECISION_1: Step 6 prompt-contract clarification — include in this fix or defer?
**Resolution**: Defer Step 6 prompt-contract clarification unless code audit produces textual evidence of a specific mis-trigger in SKILL.md anti-halt or Step 6 prose
**Disposition**: voted
**Vote tally**: THESIS=2, ANTI_THESIS=1
**Thesis summary**: Defends DEFER. The anti-halt rule at SKILL.md:30 enumerates the final transition as `5c.8→6` only — not `6→0` — and is subordinate to explicit control-flow directives, so prompt-text edits cannot stop the runtime re-entry causes (stray wakeup, delayed agent reply) that the defensive guard targets directly.
**Antithesis summary**: Defends ALWAYS-INCLUDE. The local Step 6 prose at SKILL.md:1025–1035 has no terminal "stop here" sentence after `cleanup-tmpdir.sh`, while the same paragraph at line 30 says the anti-halt rule "applies to ALL step boundaries from Step 0 through Step 6"; an explicit Step 6 terminal-boundary sentence is consistent with `orchestrator-never.md:5` global doctrine and is a cheap, locally complete clarification.
**Why thesis prevails**: Two of three judges credited the THESIS evidence that SKILL.md:30 already subordinates anti-halt to explicit control-flow directives and that no `6→0` directive exists anywhere on the path. The ANTI_THESIS strongest concession (no `6→0` directive locally) is acknowledged, but the THESIS counter holds: a missing local terminal sentence does not equal a positive instruction to re-enter, and the dissenting judge's concern about "implicit pressure to continue past Step 6" is best addressed by the Pre-Step-0 defensive guard (which catches re-entry regardless of cause) rather than by speculative prompt edits without an identified mis-trigger phrase. Step 2b must engage this antithesis by recording in the audit findings whether any phrase in SKILL.md anti-halt or Step 6 prose plausibly directs re-entry; if such a phrase is found, the surgical Step 6 clarification SHALL be added in this design instead of deferred.

### DECISION_2: Invocation telemetry — add it or rely on code audit only?
**Resolution**: Code audit only; no new telemetry in /design
**Disposition**: voted
**Vote tally**: THESIS=3, ANTI_THESIS=0
**Thesis summary**: Defends CODE-AUDIT-ONLY. The reporter-confirmed re-fire window occurs after `larch-logs/design/<RUN_ID>/` is flushed and `$DESIGN_TMPDIR` is removed, so any committed in-repo telemetry would mostly describe the completed first run while expanding /design's footprint in the direction opposite the reporter's stated concern.
**Antithesis summary**: Defends ADD-TELEMETRY. Boundary hooks at Step 0 and Step 6 reusing existing `timing-ledger.sh` / `larch-log.sh` machinery (with run id, issue, argv hash, PID lineage, parent skill, terminal outcome) plus an audit helper grouping `larch-logs/design/*/manifest.json` would buy repeatable re-entry forensics if the bug or a variant recurs, addressing the long-tail of investigative cost.
**Why thesis prevails**: All three judges credited the THESIS finding that committed design telemetry is architecturally blind to the confirmed re-fire window — the second entry happens after the first run's logs are flushed and before any second run reaches its own Step 6 flush. The ANTI_THESIS strongest concession (no compact machine-readable timeline tying first to second invocation) is honest but unfixed by the proposed mechanism: the second run would also need to complete and flush before its telemetry surfaces, and a defensive Pre-Step-0 guard short-circuits the second run before any new telemetry rows are produced. Step 2b leaves telemetry out of scope and notes follow-up issue eligibility if the defensive guard proves insufficient over the next 30 days.
