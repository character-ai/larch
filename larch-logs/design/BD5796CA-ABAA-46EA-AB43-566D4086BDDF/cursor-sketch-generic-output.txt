The core decision is to treat this as a two-phase investigation: an audit pass to identify which of the suspected re-entry mechanisms (anti-halt mis-trigger, stray ScheduleWakeup, /loop sentinel leakage, SendMessage reply from a subagent, or upstream harness double-call) is actually firing, paired with a defensive guard at /design entry that lands regardless of whether the audit finds a smoking gun. The audit reads the recent committed larch-logs/design/<RUN_ID>/ bundles plus any in-tmpdir breadcrumbs still on disk, looking specifically for paired turn-boundary markers around the Step 5 machine footer and Step 6 cleanup-tmpdir.sh call — i.e., did the orchestrator emit another `> **🔶 /design 0: setup**` line after the terminal footer, and if so, did it arrive in the same Claude session (PPID match) or a fresh one. The guard is placed at the very top of skills/design/SKILL.md Pre-Step-0 (before the existing tier/partition argv gate so it cannot be skipped by an early-exit path) and is mirrored in scripts/lib-title-eligibility.sh (or a new sibling like scripts/lib-design-reentry-guard.sh) so the title-prefix check and the new session-cache check share a single grammar source. The guard prints a single prominent banner naming which of the three sub-guards fired — lifecycle (the existing ^\[(IMPLEMENTING|DONE|DESIGNING|DESIGNED)\] rule in lib-title-eligibility.sh), larch:plan (the existing in-body block detected via scripts/plan-block-read.sh), or session-cache (a new check: a small marker file like ~/.cache/larch/sessions/design-completed-<PPID>-<ISSUE_NUMBER> written at the end of Step 5c when PLAN_WRITE_OK=true and read at Pre-Step-0, with a short TTL so legitimate re-runs after a few minutes are not blocked).

The concrete edit surface is narrow. skills/design/SKILL.md Pre-Step-0 grows a single Bash block that sources the new helper and bails with a `**⚠ /design: refusing re-entry — guard=<lifecycle|larch:plan|session-cache> reason=...**` banner before session-setup.sh runs (preserving the no-DESIGN_TMPDIR invariant the existing tier-mutual-exclusion gate already follows). scripts/lib-title-eligibility.sh either gains a new design_reentry_session_cache_hit() function or a new sibling helper scripts/lib-design-reentry-guard.sh is added with its required .md sibling per the script-md-siblings.md rule. Step 5c item 10 (the rename-to-[DESIGNED] block around line 975) gains one extra command — write the session-cache marker — gated identically on SESSION_ID non-empty and PUBLISH_OK=true, and Step 6 cleanup leaves the marker in place (the cache directory is outside $DESIGN_TMPDIR, so cleanup-tmpdir.sh does not touch it). The regression harness lives at skills/design/scripts/test-design-reentry-guard.sh (with its .md sibling and a make target wired through the existing test-design-* pattern), driving three fixtures: a fresh title (passes), a [DESIGNED]-prefixed title (lifecycle hits), and a title that the audit clears but for which a marker file exists for the same PPID+issue (session-cache hits). The anti-halt continuation reminder at SKILL.md line 30 is deliberately not edited unless the audit produces textual evidence of a mis-trigger — Round 1 decision #3 — so the harness must not assert on its content.

The chief tradeoff is that the defensive guard is a partial fix layered over an unknown root cause: if the audit returns inconclusive (the most likely outcome given that logs are flushed before re-fire), the guard prevents user-visible recurrence but does not explain the mechanism, and any future change to the anti-halt reminder, Step 5 footer, or Step 6 cleanup could regress in a way the guard silently absorbs. The Codex sketch's "explicit terminal boundary at Step 6" framing is plausible but probably mis-attributed: the anti-halt reminder is paired with `proceed to Step N+1` directives and the cleanup section already has no "continue to Step 7" instruction, so the reminder text per se does not direct re-entry into Step 0 — turning Step 6 into a louder terminal marker would not stop a stray ScheduleWakeup or a delayed SendMessage reply, which are equally plausible causes and live outside SKILL.md prose entirely. The session-cache sub-guard carries the risk of being too aggressive (refusing a legitimate operator re-run within the TTL window after a [DESIGNED] rename has been manually reverted), which is mitigated by the prominent breadcrumb naming the guard and a documented escape hatch (delete the marker file, or wait out the TTL); the lifecycle and larch:plan sub-guards already exist and are not changing, so they inherit no new risk. The bounded scope (one skill, one helper, one harness, one untouched anti-halt rule) keeps the blast radius small enough that a follow-up audit-driven fix can land independently without unwinding this work.
