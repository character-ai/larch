### In-Scope Findings

1. **Blocking**. `correctness`. `<TMPDIR>/plan.txt:145-154`
   **What:** Apply-time revalidation is too narrow. It revalidates rewrites and closes, but it does not require re-normalizing dependency edges against current titles and current dependency edges before calling `block-issue`.
   **Breakage path:** A planned REGULAR client can be renamed to `[IMPLEMENTING]` after planning. `deps apply` can still call `block-issue`, and `python/issue_block.py` only validates positive issue numbers before mutating the client issue. See `plan.txt:100-104`, `plan.txt:257-265`, and `python/issue_block.py:54-67,101-121`.
   **Suggested fix:** In `apply`, re-fetch current titles and dependency edges. Re-run flip, duplicate, self-edge, and cycle validation for each approved edge. Skip and warn if the normalized client is now DESIGNING, DESIGNED, or IMPLEMENTING.

2. **Important**. `correctness`. `<TMPDIR>/plan.txt:33-37`
   **What:** The flip rule is unconditional when `X` is in-flight and `Y` is REGULAR. The feature and discussion both say flipping must happen only “if reasonable,” but the plan schema has only `client_issue`, `blocker_issue`, `confidence`, and `reason`, with no `flip_allowed` or dependency kind. See `feature-description.txt:3-5`, `discussion-round1.md:31-34`, and `plan.txt:79-82`.
   **Breakage path:** If `[IMPLEMENTING] #10` truly needs REGULAR `#11` to land first, the plan flips this to `#11 blocked by #10`, which records the opposite dependency and can block the prerequisite work.
   **Suggested fix:** Add an explicit `flip_safe` or dependency-kind field. Only flip serialization or conflict edges where reversing the blocked issue is semantically valid. Skip and warn for hard-prerequisite edges whose client is in-flight.
