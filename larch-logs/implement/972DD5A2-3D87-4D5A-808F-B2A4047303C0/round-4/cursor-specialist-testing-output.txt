### In-Scope Findings

1. **Important** (`risk-integration`) — [`scripts/test-harness-timer.sh:60-66`](scripts/test-harness-timer.sh:60-66): The `sleep 2` assertion uses `^[12]\.[0-9]{2}s$`, which only accepts timings from 1.00s through 2.99s. On a loaded CI host, `sleep 2` can legitimately finish at 3.00s or higher wall time, so the harness would fail even though `harness-timer.sh` behaved correctly. Widen the check (for example numeric range `>= 1.8 && <= 4.0` with the same two-decimal shape, or allow a leading digit class that includes `3` with an upper cap).

2. **Latent** (`risk-integration`) — [`scripts/test-harness-timer.sh:51-57`](scripts/test-harness-timer.sh:51-57): The `sleep 0.5` regex floor is `0.40s`. A fast or imprecise scheduler could occasionally yield `0.39s`, failing the test despite acceptable behavior. Lower risk than the `sleep 2` ceiling; consider a slightly wider band or a small numeric range like the `false` case.

3. **Nit** (`code-quality`) — [`scripts/test-harness-timer.sh:9-20`](scripts/test-harness-timer.sh:9-20): The failure helper is named `fail` while the counter variable is also `fail`, which is easy to misread and brittle for future edits. Rename the helper (for example `record_fail`) or the counter variable to avoid the collision.

None identified; shard coverage logic enumerates `test-*` targets and prerequisites without hardcoding this harness name, and the diff wires `test-harness-timer` into [`Makefile`](Makefile) shard 12 and [`agent-lint.toml`](agent-lint.toml) consistently with peer harnesses.
