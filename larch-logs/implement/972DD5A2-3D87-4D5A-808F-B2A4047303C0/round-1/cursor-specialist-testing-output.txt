### In-Scope Findings

1. **Latent** (`risk-integration`): [`scripts/test-harness-timer.sh`](scripts/test-harness-timer.sh) `27-33` — The required `^[12]\.[0-9]{2}s$` check rejects a timing of `3.00s` or higher. On a very slow or heavily contended runner, `sleep 2` plus Python/timer overhead can plausibly reach or exceed three seconds wall time, so the harness could flake while the implementation is still correct. Relax the upper bound (for example allow `3\.[0-9]{2}` or a bounded range like the 0.5s case) or assert a minimum duration with a generous ceiling.

2. **Latent** (`risk-integration`): [`scripts/test-harness-timer.sh`](scripts/test-harness-timer.sh) `18-25` — The sleep 0.5 check caps the reported value at `0.69s` (`^0\.[4-6][0-9]s$`). A reported `0.70s` fails even though the slop narrative says about ±100 ms around 0.5s; large scheduler jitter could produce a rare false failure. Widen the tenths digit or match a numeric range instead of a single-decimal-tenths class.

3. **Latent** (`risk-integration`): [`scripts/harness-timer.sh`](scripts/harness-timer.sh) `8-13` — Every timed harness invocation now starts three `python3` processes (was two cheap `date` calls). Across hundreds of Makefile recipes this adds non-trivial fork/exec and interpreter startup cost and can lengthen CI without changing functional behavior. Mitigation: one `python3` snippet that reads start/end and prints elapsed, or a tiny helper module; only worth it if shard budgets regress.

4. **Nit** (`risk-integration` / `plan`): [`scripts/test-harness-timer.sh`](scripts/test-harness-timer.sh) `1-16` — The written plan asked for the same scaffolding as some peers (`REPO_ROOT`, `cleanup_tmpdir`); this harness needs neither (no temp dirs, script-local `TIMER`). Optional alignment only; no functional gap.
